# Task-Family Search Guide

Read only the relevant section when choosing baselines or improvement mechanisms. These are conditional experiment choices, not fixed algorithm prescriptions or implemented adapters. For score provenance and validity requirements, use [the adapter contract](adapter-contract.md); for experiment decisions, use [the search protocol](score-search.md).

## Data and Model Competitions

For unweighted scalar regression or binary CSV predictions, [the local prediction tools](data-model.md) provide RMSE, MAE, binary log loss, ROC-AUC, matched score and row/group sampling-uncertainty comparisons, development error/slice diagnostics, and fixed-weight blending with reusable recipes. Other metrics or aggregation rules require a task-specific adapter.

Identify the statistical unit and intended test conditions before training. Determine whether samples share patients/users/devices, follow time order, or contain duplicates; choose group/time separation where appropriate. Check distribution shifts using features permitted by the rules. Keep target-dependent feature generation inside training folds.

For explicit row-ID partitions, [audit the declared split](validation-split.md) before fitting, adding group/time/label-availability metadata only when required by the task. Reuse matching receipts across candidates; they do not prove actual pipeline fitting or independence.

Inspect loader/cache preprocessing and retain fitting-row and pipeline lineage as described in [training and validation evidence](data-model.md#establish-training-and-validation-evidence). Small held-out sets can reverse model rankings; resolve close comparisons with affordable matched internal folds or seeds when that would change the next proposal. Freeze a delivery rule for confirmation disagreements rather than selecting a new rule from held-out outcomes.

Build a cheap sanity baseline and, when resources allow, a credible model from a different family suggested by task evidence. Compare the official metric from aligned validation predictions; the training loss or accuracy may not be the contest objective. Interpret model rankings jointly with feature/data quality and error regimes.

[Graphormer](cross-competition-cases.md#kdd-cup-2021-encode-relationships-between-nodes) | Matched training and split, with runtime and memory by graph size |

[Waymo](cross-competition-cases.md#waymo-2022-cover-distinct-trajectory-modes) | Official score and misses by outcome and horizon on whole held-out scenes |

| Clear regions establish an entity's identity but ambiguous regions confuse local predictions | Test refinement from reliable parts of the same input where adaptation is permitted. | Models have complementary errors or a source demonstrates an applicable integration | Choose a fixed blend, prediction-only stack or integration with inference context using [the ensemble guide](prediction-ensembles.md) | Matched scorer and base predictions, fold-local fitting for a learned combiner, and total inference cost |
| Training is stable but the metric depends on output interpretation | Inspect output mapping and implement a justified calibration or decoding change | Development-only selection and final confirmation under the official metric |
| High-scoring predictions often compete to match the same event | Derive marginal coverage from the scorer's tolerances. Replay it against peak ranking or a simple suppression rule. [Detect Sleep States](cross-competition-cases.md#detect-sleep-states-score-the-next-event-by-uncovered-mass) | Same held-out sequences, predictions and output count. | Local predictions look plausible but reconstructed routes are disconnected or too short | Replay graph decoding on fixed predictions and inspect route costs separately. [SpaceNet 5](cross-competition-cases.md#spacenet-5-diagnose-connectivity-and-route-costs) | Matched reference graphs, false shortcuts, reachability and the official route score |
| High confidence false positives hurt a score that ranks predictions across queries | Test confidence on fixed predictions and test possible label corrections separately. [Google Landmark](cross-competition-cases.md#google-landmark-separate-label-and-confidence-changes) | Pooled scoring on a matched mix of known and unknown queries |
| The scorer takes a mean and a standard deviation | Replay fixed means and calibrate the predicted standard deviations. [Ariel](cross-competition-cases.md#ariel-calibrate-uncertainty-with-a-fixed-mean) | Disjoint calibration and scored groups under the official likelihood and normalization |
| Inference inputs rule out part of a predicted distribution | Restrict the output to its proven support. [NFL's CDF tails](cross-competition-cases.md#nfl-big-data-bowl-2020-bounded-distributions) | Bound and endpoint validity, plus matched rescoring on the same predictions |
| Point estimates drift and another observation measures relative motion | Compare smoothing alone with time-aligned displacement constraints. [Google Smartphone Decimeter](cross-competition-cases.md#google-smartphone-decimeter-anchor-position-and-displacement) | Whole-trajectory holdouts, motion and missingness slices, and legal time access |
| Recording labels cover a longer period than scored predictions | Compare input context and neighbor score aggregation separately. [BirdCLEF](cross-competition-cases.md#birdclef-match-context-to-the-scored-window) | Complete recording holdouts with window truth and the official classwise metric |

Use the simplest complete pipeline that tests the proposal. Inspect the current pipeline, learning behavior and historical comparisons to identify missing signal, representation limits or wasted work; implement the next supported mechanism without a default hyperparameter/seed sweep. Final retraining on all permitted labeled data must keep the selected pipeline fixed and satisfy inference limits; preserve the earlier validation evidence rather than claiming a new full-data validation score.

### Choose a Tabular Training Mechanism

**Aggregate repeated fields by type.** First check whether slot order carries information. When slots are interchangeable, map their category codes to a shared vocabulary, sum the values by type, and keep the total when scale matters. Type shares can describe composition where the total is positive. Handle structural absence separately from unknown values. Swap complete `(type, value)` pairs through the actual feature path to check invariance before fitting. Compare the grouped and slot representations with the same learner and folds, inspecting rows with multiple occupied slots and rows with structural absence. Jack Roberts v8 sums basement area by finish type and computes the shares of total basement area. Its public `0.11553` is for the full submission. [Jack Roberts v8](https://www.kaggle.com/code/jack89roberts/top-7-using-elasticnet-with-interactions?scriptVersionId=3823933)

**Limit interaction search with an explicit assumption.** When a full expansion is too wide, start with domain-supported pairs or screen a smaller pool. Jack Roberts v8 screens main effects with ElasticNet, ranks roughly 700 resulting candidate interactions with Lasso, then refits ElasticNet with 20 of them. Main-effect screening favors interactions that accompany a marginal signal. It misses, for example, `y = x1 * x2` for independent equally likely signs `x1, x2 ∈ {-1, 1}`, where both marginal effects vanish. Compare main effects alone with main effects plus the selected interactions. Fit learned preprocessing, screening and refitting inside the training partition at each comparison level. [Jack Roberts v8](https://www.kaggle.com/code/jack89roberts/top-7-using-elasticnet-with-interactions?scriptVersionId=3823933)

**Share information while retaining original categories.** Compare the original categories with those same inputs plus a coarser group when related categories have little data. With complete category indicators, a fixed group indicator is a sum of those columns. It can change regularization in a linear model or give a tree a single split that pools several categories. For groups derived from targets, estimate the mapping within the training partition, cross-fit the training-row encodings, and define a fallback for unseen categories. If a published mapping used labels in your validation folds, treat it as a design lead and re-estimate it locally. Humananalog v1 keeps the original neighborhoods alongside tiers based on median price. Its public `0.11623` covers the full submission. [Humananalog v1](https://www.kaggle.com/code/humananalog/xgboost-lasso?scriptVersionId=423092) · [Supervised preprocessing checks](tabular-training.md#verify-supervised-preprocessing-information-flow)

**Synthetic prior or generated training data.** A pretrained tabular model can transfer a prior learned from synthetic tasks. Generating examples for this contest changes what the learner sees during fitting. Choose based on the gap in the current model and the match between the prior or generator and the task's sample size, feature types and missingness. Check that the checkpoint or source data are permitted. For a checkpoint substitution, compare with a strong incumbent on the same feature view and outer folds. That measures the replacement as a whole. If adding generated rows, compare the same learner with and without those rows. Fit any task-specific generator and draw source examples within each training partition. Keep derived examples with their source groups and score on the original validation rows. Account for fit and inference cost. Artola's House Prices notebook uses the released TabPFN v2 model with the competition's labeled records as its fit context. [TabPFN v2 paper](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-024-08328-6) · [Artola's notebook](https://github.com/artola94/HousePrices-AdvancedRegression/blob/8b0bb217268592a419cae6ba481dea3564d0e387/nb_HousePrices.ipynb)

**Target offset.** In the modeled target space `T(y)`, specify a baseline `b(z)` computable at inference, fit `T(y) - b(z)`, and add `b(z)` back before evaluation and delivery. Check what becomes easier for the learner. If its unregularized linear function space contains `b`, both constructions can represent exactly the same predictions. For a known varying offset, compare it against direct prediction with the same features, learner and folds. For a learned offset, fit it within the outer training partition. When the residual learner is supposed to correct errors on unseen examples, generate the residuals from inner out-of-fit predictions. Verify that any external context was permitted and available at the prediction date. Harless subtracts the log of a regional price index to learn relative prices and adds it back for the prediction. [House Prices source](https://github.com/andyharless/ames/blob/39f80e4cd0d78b838d5ff3b9b33729f6e26516c1/house6.R)

**Anomalous measurements and hard rows.** Distinguish a documented absence, such as no garage, from an unknown value. Distinguish implausible measurements from valid rare cases before deciding to impute, use a robust loss or downweight a training row. Fit the treatment within each training partition. A treatment applied at inference must use only available inputs. Score every row in the same declared validation set, including hard cases, and inspect paired error contributions on relevant slices. A validation score after removing hard cases estimates a different population. Mateusz's `OutliersSoftener` replaces flagged measurements while keeping the rows. Van Elteren reports a worse result after deleting training outliers in his pipeline. These motivate paired comparisons of the specific treatment. [Mateusz v10](https://www.kaggle.com/code/mateuszk013/house-prices-eda-machine-learning?scriptVersionId=120546918) · [Van Elteren's report](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/house-prices-advanced-regression-techniques/discussion/170472)

When genuine extremes in one input may have too much influence, compare its raw value with a capped view `min(x, c)` using the same learner, folds and rows. Use a task-based cutoff or estimate it inside each training partition, then apply the same rule at inference. Count affected rows and inspect their paired errors alongside the full validation score. Values above the cutoff become indistinguishable in that input. If the excess may carry signal, test a separate term such as `log1p(max(x - c, 0))` alongside the cap. Carlos Farfán's notebook caps lot area at 45,000 square feet, affecting 13 official training rows and five test rows. His `0.11564` is author-reported for the complete submission, and the score-labeled CSV differs from the repository's current `submission.csv`. [Author report](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/house-prices-advanced-regression-techniques/discussion/384549) · [Pinned notebook, cell 37](https://github.com/carlosfarfangalindoNL222/Learning-Machine-Learning-with-House_Prices_Challenge/blob/5f2734567a12424658d77cac080f9024259dbbdb/Code/House_Price_newest.ipynb)

## LLM Generation and Agent Competitions

When the submitted system trains or runs a language model, use the relevant section of [the LLM/agent playbook](llm-agent-competitions.md). It connects validation and scoring behavior, synthetic data and teacher distillation, post-training, inference budgets, trajectory replay and deterministic agent components to the existing implementation/evaluation loop. An LLM writing a conventional contest algorithm does not by itself require this playbook.

Choose a mechanism from observed failures and the task's constraints. Preserve model/tokenizer/template and data lineage, compare the complete system under the actual inference limit, and distinguish cached-trajectory diagnostics from a new executed policy. The bundled numeric training and CSV prediction tools do not supply an LLM trainer, weight merger or interactive evaluator.

## Algorithms and Combinatorial Optimization

Distinguish constructing a solution, improving it offline, making online decisions, and accelerating an exact solver. Inspect input scale, allowed operations, and the scorer. On tiny instances, a slow exact or exhaustive reference can expose quality gaps even when it cannot serve as the submitted algorithm.

Start from a valid constructive/reference solution. Use per-instance objective components, constraint violations, search progress, representative outputs, and profiles to locate the limiting mechanism. When available, inspect the official visualizer or a trace; a final scalar alone may hide poor neighborhoods or expensive score updates.

| Observation | Experiment to consider | Confirmation needed |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Initial constructions have very different quality | Explore another construction or representation | Gains on representative full-scale cases |
| Local search stalls with substantial estimated headroom | Change neighborhoods, restarts, annealing, beam search, or decomposition as task evidence suggests | Progress curves and final score under the same runtime limit |
| Local edits throw away many still-valid steps of a construction | Propagate deletions through dependencies and repair the remaining solution. [RectJoin](cross-competition-cases.md#ahc014-repair-a-legal-construction-by-dependency) | Official legality checks, repair cost and score at the same search budget |
| A legal endpoint repair is long, or the restricted move set cannot reach the target | Derive small-effect legal move compositions and their invariants. Where a fixed suffix is invertible, compare appending a repair with inserting an equivalent repair earlier. [Santa 2023](cross-competition-cases.md#santa-2023-derive-repairs-from-legal-moves) | Reuse the same starting paths and move table. Check full replay, final length and runtime under the same budget. |
| Construction order changes when cash or revenue becomes available | Replay purchase schedules with the site choices and paths fixed. [AHC043](cross-competition-cases.md#ahc043-schedule-purchases-around-delayed-revenue) | Legal balances before collection and matched final scores |
| Inner scoring dominates runtime | Use incremental updates, caching, or a candidate-internal approximation | Correct updates and final evaluation using the declared objective |
| Inputs share a total budget and benefit differently from more resources | Cache existing options, then compare uniform allocation, greedy and a budgeted solver. [CLIC](cross-competition-cases.md#clic-2019-allocate-one-budget-across-inputs) | Compare the full batch at matched actual cost using the official score |
| Exact solver time explodes on larger instances | Change encoding, auxiliary variables, symmetry treatment, or variable order | Correctness, larger-instance scaling, success rate, and the contest objective |
| A candidate conflicts with every option of another mandatory decision | Propagate deletions implied by hard constraints before search. [ITC 2019](cross-competition-cases.md#itc-2019-remove-unsupported-choices-before-search) | The same feasible solutions and objectives on tiny cases, then a comparison at the same total solver budget |
| Repeated infeasible candidates share only a subset of their decisions | Reuse an existing solver's proved conflict and compare bounded shrinking. [MaxSAT](cross-competition-cases.md#maxsat-2018-reuse-proven-conflicts) | No loss of legal solutions, and completed instances under the same total budget |
| An ordering may have fixed pair costs and locally optimal choices can conflict | Derive pair costs and check valid global orders on tiny cases. [PACE 2024](cross-competition-cases.md#pace-2024-exploit-pairwise-ordering-costs) | Agreement with the original scorer and solver comparisons under the same budget |
| Online decisions do well on averages but fail in particular sequences | Try lookahead where allowed, a revised heuristic, or a regime policy | Only information available at decision time and matched sequence evaluation |
| Strategies excel at different sizes or structures | Combine them or use an input-based selector | Selector overhead, worst allowed resource use, and aggregate objective |

Candidate speed can buy more search iterations under the contest deadline. Measure both iteration throughput and the quality obtained within the actual runtime budget; faster operations alone do not establish a higher score. Record whether search exhausted a neighborhood or hit its work/time cap, with development objective progress when useful. A weak construction may become a stronger complete construction-plus-search pipeline; compare that mechanism before rejecting it when affordable. Keep hard time/memory checks separate from soft speed/quality tradeoffs. Confirm on fresh instances/seeds and boundary sizes, respecting the official aggregation.

## Stochastic Simulation, Control, and Interactive Tasks

Identify which state or observations are available to the candidate, which actions are permitted, and how outcomes become the score. Separate hard feasibility failures from penalties that are part of the official objective. Include per-episode query/interaction costs and execution limits.

Use the [live interaction checks](adapter-contract.md#live-interaction-and-saved-output) before accepting a locally scored policy. For queries that reveal only a comparison, rank or discrete action, derive the constraints implied by the full response and tie rule. Evaluate a query policy by the decisions its evidence changes and by complete legal episodes with the same query and time budget. [AHC045 example](cases-optimization.md#atcoder-ahc045-oracle-guided-road)

Use a simple valid policy, matched environment seeds, and repeated policy seeds where applicable. Track return/score, failure rates, dispersion, and cost by scenario. A single lucky episode or low percentile does not establish the official objective if it aggregates outcomes differently.

| Observation | Experiment to consider | Confirmation needed |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Results vary widely between runs | Resolve uncertainty or test variance reduction | Matched comparisons and sufficient repetition for the decision |
| A known transition model has a small state space and the objective is an expectation | Compute the expected score with a recurrence. [AHC009](cross-competition-cases.md#ahc009-compute-the-expected-score) | Agreement with the declared scoring semantics and a matched end-to-end budget |
| Failures cluster in particular states or regimes | Revise the policy, observations, or task model | Feasibility and official penalties on representative scenarios |
| A multi-action strategy continues after a state change that calls for a different action | Locate stale decisions in trajectories and compare an interrupt rule. [NetHack](cross-competition-cases.md#nethack-interrupt-strategies-when-state-changes) | Matched starting states and budgets, full episodes, and outcomes by starting regime |
| Planning consumes most of the deadline | Change approximation, horizon, caching, or per-state compute allocation | Policy quality including all computation/query cost |
| Cheap short episodes rank policies inconsistently | Evaluate selected policies on full episodes | Matched horizons and the declared score aggregation |
| Policy improvements depend on the opponents | Compare policies on frozen lineups and reserve other styles for confirmation. [Halite](cross-competition-cases.md#halite-compare-policies-across-opponents) | Shared seeds and seats, full episodes, and results by opponent group |
| Different policies cover different regimes | Test a selector using allowed observations | Combined objective, selection overhead, and fresh confirmation scenarios |

Policy parameters, surrogate rewards, or internal planning heuristics are candidate changes; keep the official reward/evaluator fixed. Hidden future state or final outcome cannot be a selector feature. Confirm under the intended environment distribution and label sensitivity to alternative distributions as diagnostic evidence.
