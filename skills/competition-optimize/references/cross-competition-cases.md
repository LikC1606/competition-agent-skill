## M5 Forecasting Across Windows

YeonJun IN_STU finished first on the private leaderboard with WRMSSE `0.52043`. The team backtested three consecutive 28-day periods and also used its public leaderboard period during selection. The released method combined two forecasting paths and three model-sharing schemes. The recursive path predicts a day and feeds the prediction back into later rolling features. The direct path uses sales lags available at the forecast origin to predict the full horizon at once. Models share parameters within a store, store and category, or store and department. All six forecasts are aligned at the product and store level and averaged equally before scoring the hierarchy. [Official leaderboard](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/m5-forecasting-accuracy/leaderboard) · [Winner's account](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/m5-forecasting-accuracy/discussion/163684) · [Organizer's source archive at a fixed commit](https://github.com/Mcompetitions/M5-methods/tree/18e4e776cfa1ded3b387fabe008c6c2c44922007/Code%20of%20Winning%20Methods/A1)

In Ma and Fildes's independent reconstruction, the average beat every individual path in two of five windows, including the private period. In the other three, at least one individual path scored better. The released sales statistics are fitted through day 1913. Whether the original earlier backtests rebuilt them at their own forecast origins remains unresolved. [Independent comparison, Table 2](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijforecast.2021.09.002) · [Organizer's preprocessing source](https://github.com/Mcompetitions/M5-methods/blob/18e4e776cfa1ded3b387fabe008c6c2c44922007/Code%20of%20Winning%20Methods/A1/3.%20code/1.%20preprocessing/1.%20preprocessing.py#L497-L506)

Rebuild target-dependent features at each training cutoff using only information available at that origin. Reuse saved predictions if their feature cutoffs are verified and their origins, IDs and scoring weights match. Compare a direct path, a recursive path and a fixed blend, inspecting paired errors by window, lead time and hierarchy level. Late-horizon deterioration can motivate a check for error propagation. A large change by aggregation level can motivate a change in which series share parameters. Use the official aggregate to select on development windows and keep later confirmation out of blend selection.

## OTTO Candidate Coverage and Ranking

mrkmakr finished first with weighted Recall@20 `0.60503`. The solution combined previously visited items, co-visitation candidates and neural retrieval, then reranked with LightGBM. Some neural models focused on items absent from the visible session. The author's local score was `0.588359` for the full single ranker, `0.583136` with co-visitation information removed, and `0.583055` with neural information removed. Each removal affected both the candidate pool and the reranker's features, so those differences measure the full path. [Official leaderboard](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/otto-recommender-system/leaderboard) · [Winner's method and ablation](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/otto-recommender-system/writeups/mrkmakr-1st-place-solution)

For a query with candidate set `C`, relevant set `G` and output limit `k`, a ranker restricted to `C` can hit at most `min(k, |C ∩ G|)` items. For OTTO, sum that bound over queries and divide by the sum of `min(20, |G|)` for each behavior. Apply the official click, cart and order weights of `0.10`, `0.30` and `0.60`. Apply this derivation only where the scorer and the candidate restriction support it. [Official metric](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/otto-recommender-system/overview/evaluation)

Replay cached lists on the same time-held-out queries, using candidate sources built from information available at each query time. Compute the bound before and after removing each source from the union, and compare actual ranker scores to the bound by behavior. A bound below the desired score points to missing coverage. A large gap to a high bound points to ranking opportunity. Compare a new source at a comparable candidate budget or compare rankers with the candidate set held fixed. Track candidate counts and time for both stages. Measure the actual effect through the full scoring path.

## HMS Training With Annotator Votes

Team Sony ranked first on the private leaderboard with KL divergence `0.272332`. Its released training code groups folds by patient and preserves normalized vote distributions as soft targets. Some branches first fit on a broader sample and then fine-tune on higher-vote records. For kfuji's branch, the writeup reports a minimum of 10 votes in the second stage and the released training code uses 7. The exact threshold used in the original submission is unresolved. Total votes measure the number of annotations. Agreement is a property of their distribution. [Official leaderboard](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/hms-harmful-brain-activity-classification/leaderboard) · [Team and member writeup](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/hms-harmful-brain-activity-classification/discussion/492560#2744643) · [Training release v3](https://www.kaggle.com/code/sugupoko/1st-place-all-train-code?scriptVersionId=173277633) · [Official metric](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/hms-harmful-brain-activity-classification/overview/evaluation)

For a fixed soft target `p`, the KL divergence `KL(p || q)` equals the cross-entropy against `q` minus the entropy of `p`. Where that matches the metric, preserving the vote distribution lets soft cross-entropy train on the same per-record objective up to a constant. To test a second-stage data policy, resume both arms from the same checkpoint and use the same update budget. Change only the later sample pool or only its weights. Score the same held-out patients at the official unit of aggregation, and report overall KL alongside vote-count slices and their sample counts. If the gain appears only on higher-vote records, check whether the actual target population supports adopting the policy.

## Santa 2024 Screening Before Expensive Scoring

Hamiltonians placed first with average perplexity `246.81784`. Its released search ranks a large batch of legal word-order moves with a light model, then sends the top 112 and 16 randomly sampled others to Gemma for exact scoring. The seventh-place entry uses summed adjacent-word costs as a cheaper filter and also reserves random slots. The first 13 entries displayed the same score, and the rules break ties by submission time. Comparing filter gains requires a shared search budget and starting states. [Official leaderboard](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/santa-2024/leaderboard) · [Tie rule](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/santa-2024/rules) · [Winner's code at a fixed commit](https://github.com/Lgeu/santa2024/blob/9cdebc2c360e1f977343f9e1deb16fc4d952bc9e/code_nagiss/main.py#L482-L559) · [Seventh-place writeup](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/santa-2024/writeups/miner2024-7th-place-solution-sa-sa-sa)

Hold the starting states, legal moves and exact scorer fixed. Replay the same scored neighborhoods for each filter. If the cache covers only candidates admitted by the old filter, score a small complete neighborhood or a shared sample drawn independently of that filter. Under the same number of exact calls, compare the fraction of improving candidates admitted within the replay pool and the best exact score found. Include a simple filter or unguided sample as a control. If the replay supports the new filter, compare complete searches from matched starts at the same exact-call or wall-time budget, as the task requires. ## Feedback Prize Span Decoding and Fusion

world peace ranked first with macro F1 `0.753`. Its system generates candidate spans from word-level scores and ranks spans in a second stage. The official rule requires the shared word count to cover at least half of both the prediction and the annotation within the same essay and class. A candidate's overlap with another prediction is a separate decoding choice. The third-place bestoverfitting submission at `0.740` scores spans by class and greedily accepts a candidate that meets the class threshold if at most 15% of its words are covered by the union of already accepted same-class spans. The fixed notebook version's submission ID matches the private result. [Official evaluation](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/feedback-prize-2021/overview/evaluation) · [Official leaderboard](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/feedback-prize-2021/leaderboard) · [Winner's writeup](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/feedback-prize-2021/writeups/world-peace-1st-solution-with-code-cv-0-748-lb-0-7) · [Third-place submission v2, cell 25](https://www.kaggle.com/code/aerdem4/xgb-lgb-feedback-prize-cv-0-7322?scriptVersionId=90101694)

Feedforward's second-place release at `0.740` clusters overlapping spans of the same class in common word coordinates and averages their endpoints by confidence. The scored v4 requires support from at least two model sources. The sixth-place team reported no gain from weighted endpoint fusion in its setup. Use the candidate source and decoder together when interpreting such results. [Feedforward v4](https://www.kaggle.com/code/cdeotte/2nd-place-solution-cv741-public727-private740?scriptVersionId=90301836) · [Sixth-place writeup](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/feedback-prize-2021/writeups/rist-tascj-6th-place-solution-a-yolo-like-text-spa)

Replay saved out-of-fold spans and scores with the same essays, class thresholds and tie order. Compare strict suppression with limited overlap and count the added true and false positives by class alongside macro F1. A one-word boundary overshoot can suppress a second valid span under strict suppression. Use the same scorer version for both arms and inspect duplicate matches when they affect the result. The platform's duplicate-match handling remains unresolved. When different models agree on class and differ on endpoints, compare boundary fusion using their same saved outputs.

## ARC Prize 2024 Selecting From Multiple Views

The ARChitects took first place among the open-source prize winners with `53.5%` on the private evaluation. Each queried output grid gets two attempts, with credit for an exact match on either. The team generates candidates with probability-guided depth-first search across reversible views of a task, maps the outputs back to the original grid, and re-ranks distinct grids. Its paper evaluates a candidate's log probability across jointly rotated, reflected or recolored task and answer grids and sums the scores. The organizer-linked notebook also incorporates generation scores. It adapts a LoRA model using the supplied demonstrations and leaves the additional single-task retraining option off by default. [Organizer results](https://arcprize.org/competitions/2024) · [Official evaluation](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/arc-prize-2024/overview/evaluation) · [Author paper, sections 3.5–3.6](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/da-fr/arc-prize-2024/main/the_architects.pdf) · [Fixed solution notebook](https://www.kaggle.com/code/gregkamradt/arc-prize-v8?scriptVersionId=211457842)

In the paper's Nemo-mix experiment on 100 held-out public tasks, the candidate pool reached `80.0%` coverage. Alternative scoring on that pool raised the two-guess result from `60.5%` to `72.5%`. Preliminary fitting used the other 300 tasks from the same public evaluation set, and the authors flag possible overlap in concepts. Separately, second-place Guillermo Barbadillo reports that his learned selector scored `81.0%` versus voting's `70.0%` in one offline setting, with no gain on the leaderboard. Test a selector on candidates produced by the intended generator and adaptation policy. [Author paper, Tables 5–6](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/da-fr/arc-prize-2024/main/the_architects.pdf) · [Barbadillo's writeup](https://ironbar.github.io/arc24/05_Solution_Summary/)

Cache legal outputs in one coordinate system and hold the candidate pool and attempt budget fixed. Measure its retrospective coverage and replay the incumbent selector, voting and multi-view rescoring where transformed views preserve the task rule. Aggregate coverage and selected accuracy with the same metric weights. For tasks with multiple outputs, the published notebook divides each task's credit across its outputs. Verify the aggregation before using this evaluator for a new comparison. Low coverage favors a change to generation. A large gap from coverage to selected accuracy favors testing selection. Include rescoring cost when comparing complete systems. [Coverage and selection diagnostics](llm-agent-competitions.md#separate-coverage-from-answer-selection)

### Generate Synthetic Tasks From Executable Rules

Li et al. won the ARC paper award using seed tasks with executable input generators and transformations. They synthesize new rule programs and use each program to label several inputs consistently. In one public validation setting using the same synthetic data, solving via program generation scored `18.78%`, direct grid prediction scored `15.25%`, and their combination scored `26.50%` with the paper's decoding budgets. These results support testing distinct reasoning routes under that setup. ## Home Credit Stability Across Future Weeks

Home Credit scores discrimination and stability jointly. The organizer's public starter calculates `G_w = 2 × AUC_w − 1` per week, fits a line to the ordered indices of observed weeks, and computes `mean(G_w) + 88 × min(0, slope) − 0.5 × std(residuals)`. Weeks are weighted equally, and the code uses population standard deviation. The first-place `0.60549` writeup discloses a submission score adjustment. The 57th-place Nesterov entry scored `0.52849`. Its author describes the final blend as free of that adjustment and reports that weaker models without date inputs complemented the ensemble. The same writeup reports using public scores to set noise during stacking, and gives no isolated gain for the date-free branch. ## NFL Big Data Bowl 2020 Bounded Distributions

The task scored a 199-threshold cumulative distribution of rushing yards with CRPS. Deadline Fighter placed second on the final private leaderboard with `0.012115`. In the scored notebook v27, each model applies softmax to its yardage logits and cumulatively sums the probabilities to produce a CDF. ## PACE 2024 Exploit Pairwise Ordering Costs

PACE 2024's Exact Track fixed one layer of a bipartite graph and asked for an order of the other that minimizes straight-line edge crossings. For any two free vertices `u` and `v`, the cost of `u` preceding `v` is the count of their edge pairs whose fixed endpoints come in the opposite order. The total is a sum of these pair costs. Champion mppeg (official score `199`) used a linear ordering model, split blocks with disjoint neighbor spans and added violated cycle constraints as needed. The authors found multiple blocks in 51 of 100 public exact instances. Their full solver still timed out on one of the 100. Runner-up pingpong formulated the penalty for reversing cheaper pair directions as a weighted feedback arc set and generated cycle constraints as needed. Its authors report only minor gains from more elaborate initial cycle generation. [Official task](https://pacechallenge.org/2024/) · [Official results](https://pacechallenge.org/2024/results) · [Champion's solver description](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/pauljngr/PACE2024/main/mppegsolverdescription.pdf) · [Runner-up's solver description](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/mwien/pingpong/main/description.pdf)
